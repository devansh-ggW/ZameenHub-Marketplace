# Indian legal research notes — ZameenHub

**Research date:** 13 November 2026  
**Purpose:** Implementation guidance for a property-listing marketplace with seller-submitted legal evidence, user reviews, community discussion and user reports. This is an engineering research note, not a legal opinion.

## Executive findings

1. The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 has phased commencement. Under G.S.R. 843(E) dated 13 November 2025, section 6(9) and section 27(1)(d) commenced one year later, on 13 November 2026. Sections 3–5, sections 6(1)–(8) and 6(10), sections 7–17, section 27 except clause (d), sections 28–34, 36–37 and section 44(2) are scheduled for 13 May 2027. As of this research date, the one-year provisions have commenced; most operational personal-data provisions have not yet commenced. Recheck for later Gazette notifications before relying on this timetable.
2. The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 also use phased commencement. Rules 1, 2 and 17–21 commenced on Gazette publication; Rule 4 commenced one year later, on 13 November 2026; Rules 3, 5–16, 22 and 23 are scheduled for 13 May 2027. Rule 4 concerns consent-manager registration and duties; whether it applies to ZameenHub depends on whether it undertakes that role. Read G.S.R. 846(E) with corrigendum G.S.R. 892(E), 11 December 2025. Do not rely on the earlier draft rules.
3. The Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021, read with G.S.R. 120(E) dated 10 February 2026 and corrigendum G.S.R. 148(E) dated 26 February 2026, contain layered intermediary duties. If ZameenHub is an intermediary within scope, Rule 3(1)(c) requires user information at least once every three months; Rule 3(1)(d) contains a three-hour deadline for specified unlawful information after actual knowledge by court order or reasoned government intimation; Rule 3(2)(a) requires grievance acknowledgment within 24 hours and resolution within seven days, with a 36-hour route for specified removal grievances; Rule 3(2)(b) sets a two-hour response for complaints about specified intimate, nudity, sexual-act or impersonation content. These are distinct triggers, not one generic takedown period. The 21 April 2026 MeitY material titled as draft amendments is a draft and should not be treated as enacted law unless followed by a Gazette notification. Whether and how these duties apply to ZameenHub depends on its function and legal classification.
4. The Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 apply broadly to marketplace models and contain marketplace-entity information, seller-information, fair-practice and grievance requirements. Rule 4 includes public display of the e-commerce entity's legal name, principal geographic address and grievance/customer-care contacts, and acknowledgment of consumer complaints within 48 hours with redress within one month. The operator must assess whether the actual property-listing/lead model falls within the applicable scope.
5. Section 9 of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 is activity-specific: it restricts a real estate agent from facilitating or acting on behalf of a person to facilitate sale/purchase of a plot, apartment or building in a project registered under section 3 without agent registration. It is not a blanket statement that every property-listing website must register, and not a blanket exemption for a website either. Product conduct, remuneration, agency, lead handling and project/promoter context need Maharashtra counsel review.
6. CERT-In's 28 April 2022 directions require specified cyber incidents to be reported within six hours of noticing/being informed, and ICT system logs to be retained securely for a rolling 180 days within Indian jurisdiction. The actual hosting, logging, access and retention arrangements must be checked; a policy statement alone does not establish compliance.
7. Maharashtra's official land-records portals expose services including digitally signed 7/12, 8A, mutation (Ferfar), property cards, archived e-records and court-case status links. These are useful source routes, but a particular extract or search result is not a universal certificate of clear title or absence of claims.

## Specific product safeguards derived from the research

- Label seller-provided records as seller-submitted. Store document category, source/date/authority/jurisdiction when supplied, and whether the seller confirms authorization to publish. Do not imply ZameenHub authenticated the record.
- A 7/12 extract, property card, encumbrance certificate, court search or seller declaration has a limited scope and date. Present its source and date. Never conclude that one item proves sole ownership or that there are no disputes.
- For inherited property, avoid a universal checklist. Applicable heirship, probate, succession, release, family settlement and consent evidence depends on personal law, title history and the property jurisdiction. A document upload alone does not establish sale authority.
- Public document URLs are public. The current ImageKit/external-link architecture does not provide private-document access control for unredacted PDFs. Therefore require redacted, seller-authorized public material; do not promise confidentiality for a publicly linked file.
- Do not show reporter identities or confidential report narratives publicly. Show aggregate eligible-report counts and explicitly describe them as allegations unless substantiated.
- Do not allow seller accounts to contribute reviews to their own listing or be counted as independent customers. Keep owner replies visually separate from reviews.
- Treat public ratings as customer-experience indicators, not legal status, ownership verification, title clearance, or a promise of safe transactions.
- A privacy-policy page should reflect the actual data flows (Cloudflare Workers/D1, ImageKit, Resend, browser cookies, external links and any analytics/fonts). Do not claim encryption, deletion, localization, or retention unless tested against the deployed system and provider settings.

## Open legal questions requiring an Indian lawyer

1. Identify the legal operator (entity name, incorporation/registration details, principal address), contracting entity, governing law, jurisdiction and legal notice address.
2. Determine whether the actual platform is an e-commerce entity/marketplace under the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 and what seller onboarding/contract and grievance disclosures are required.
3. Determine whether particular ZameenHub activities amount to acting as or facilitating a RERA real-estate agent under section 9, including Maharashtra-specific MahaRERA regulations and any activity- or project-specific exceptions.
4. Classify the service under the IT Act/IT Rules and identify the applicable Grievance Officer, any nodal contact, three-hour, 36-hour and two-hour content-removal triggers, quarterly user notices and appeal channels. Read the 10 February 2026 amendments with the 26 February 2026 corrigendum and monitor later Gazette notifications.
5. Confirm application of the DPDP Act provisions and Rule 4 that commenced on 13 November 2026, and prepare for the 13 May 2027 commencement of sections 3–17 and Rules 3, 5–16, 22 and 23.
6. Approve retention schedules for accounts, KYC-like property evidence, reports, moderation/audit history, messages, abuse-prevention logs and account-deletion requests; resolve conflicts between deletion requests and legal/security retention.
7. Approve age/eligibility controls, consent and privacy notices for any child/minor users, taking account of the IT Rules' child definition and future DPDP child-related provisions.
8. Verify CERT-In logging location/retention and incident reporting procedures against Cloudflare, ImageKit, Resend and other actual processors/subprocessors.
9. Review inherited-property disclosures, Maharashtra land/revenue records, co-heir/consent statements and the precise public/private evidence design.
10. Review limitation, defamation, user-generated-content moderation, evidence preservation, intermediary and consumer-law provisions to ensure the Terms are fair and do not attempt to contract out of non-excludable liability.
